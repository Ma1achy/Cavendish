## 1. What the owner can newly do

- **Put a solid in the scene.** A body can be a shape: a sphere, a cuboid, a cylinder, an
  ellipsoid, any of the five Platonic solids, a convex polyhedron from its vertices, or a closed
  mesh from an `.obj` or `.stl` file. It is voxelised into a tree at a stated pitch and read
  exactly: every leaf a uniform cube, no point approximation.
- **See it as a tree.** A voxel body is drawn as its leaves (merged cubes at their true size),
  coloured by tree level, mass, height, distance to the nearest tower, or what each leaf adds to a
  chosen tower's phase, with a legend made from what is drawn. A view of it exports as a figure,
  as SVG and PDF (vector) or PNG.
- **Look inside the tree.** Overlays draw the leaves' bounds by level and the cut a level of
  detail would take (`overlay leaves`, `overlay cut <level>`).
- **Ask where the point mass fails.** The cylinder, the shaky wall, the plate and George's
  scaffold (one mode and many) are scenes, held to George's scripts.
- **Mix instruments.** A tower can override any part of the array's instrument (height,
  separation, T, launch, cycle, kicks, …); towers with equal instruments share a reader; each
  tower has its own shots, so bundles are ragged (stored flat with offsets, and padded with a mask).
  A scene may have no towers at all.

## 2. Design in one page

### The tree (`cav_core::voxel`)

The note's recommendation, with the review's corrections, is taken as it stands:

- **A linear octree from 30-bit Morton keys** (10 bits an axis, so at most 1024 finest cells
  across one tree; the note §6: WGSL has no 64-bit integers). Leaves are a flat list in Morton
  order, each `{key, level, count, material}`; internal nodes are made from the leaves' keys by
  masking, level by level (bottom-up, no pointers), with a child mask and the index of the first
  child.
- **Leaf values are integers** (the note §1.1): `count` is how many of the `k³` sample points of
  one finest cell are inside (`k` the supersampling, 1 to 6, so `k³ ≤ 216` fits a byte), the same
  for every finest cell of a merged leaf; `material` is a palette index. The tree's mass is
  `count × 8^level × unit` with `unit = M / Σ samples`: the body's mass is the spec's, to rounding
  (§3.3). Integers make "the expanded tree equals the raster voxeliser" a bit-for-bit test.
- **Lossless merge:** eight sibling leaves with the same count and material are one leaf of the
  next level (any count, not only full cells: eight uniform cubes of equal density are one uniform
  cube). The review's point that partial cells rarely merge is measured (§7).
- **Moments per internal node, in `f64` on the CPU:** mass, centre of mass, second and third
  central moments (20 numbers), bottom-up in Morton order. The note's `f32`/float-float tiering is
  for the GPU in S5; nothing here is uploaded.
- **The frame:** a body's tree is in the body's own frame, the shape's centre at the body's
  position; the grid is centred on the shape's box. The dipole is not zeroed by moving the origin
  (M14 did, for the v1 cloud's sake); a symmetric shape's centre of mass is at the centre to
  rounding, and any other's is reported (§6).

### Shapes and `cover` (`cav_core::voxel::shape`)

A `Shape` answers `contains(point)` (is a sample point inside), `bounds()`, and `cover(box)`:
`Inside`, `Outside` or `Mixed`, conservative with a margin, so that `Inside` means every sample
point in the box is inside and `Outside` none. It also gives its analytic volume and inertia, for
the tests. Primitives answer `cover` exactly from their geometry; a convex polyhedron from its
planes at the box's corners; a mesh from a bounding-volume hierarchy of its triangles (a box that
meets no triangle is wholly in or out, and one sample decides which), and `contains` by the parity
of crossings along a row, with each shared edge tested the same way from both of its triangles so
that a closed mesh is watertight in floating point. A mesh must be closed (every edge in exactly
two triangles) or it is refused with the first open edge.

**Mesh files.** The note does not recommend a format. Wavefront OBJ (positions and faces, faces
of more than three corners as fans) and STL (binary and ASCII) are read, in-house, with no
dependency: STL gives its triangle count in its header, so the cap is checked before anything is
read; OBJ is what most tools write for a hand-made solid. Recorded as a decision (§6).

### The voxeliser

Top-down from the root: `Outside` drops the box, `Inside` emits one full leaf at that level,
`Mixed` splits, down to the finest level, where the `k³` sample points are counted with
`contains`. Children are visited in Morton order, so the leaves come out sorted. Merging is part
of the descent (a box whose eight children came back as equal leaves is replaced by one). The
**raster oracle** classifies every finest cell of the grid by the same sample points and the same
`contains`, and the tree expanded to finest cells must equal it, count for count.

### The cube kernel (`cav_core::voxel::kernel`)

A leaf is a uniform cube. Its potential is taken by the **cube series** (monopole, then the cubic
harmonics of a uniform cube: `l = 4, 6, 8, 10, 12`, their coefficients derived from the cube's
moments, `⟨x⁴⟩ = s⁴/80` and so on) when the series' **truncation bound**
`M·(a/R)^14/(R(1 − a/R))`, `a = s√3/2`, is below the rounding of the monopole, and otherwise by
the **closed-form prism** (the Nagy/MacMillan formula, with each corner's terms taken about the
nearest corner). Between two arm points the monopole is differenced without cancellation, as the
point mass is (REBUILD §3.4). The read of a voxel body is the exact sum over its leaves, in
Morton order: no node is accepted in place of its leaves (that is S5's bounded walk). A static
body reads the same at every shot, and is read once a tower.

### Bodies made of voxels (`cav-sim`)

`BodySpec` gains `shape: Option<ShapeSpec>` (`kind`, `pitch` in metres, `supersample`). With a
shape, the body is a tree of that shape and mass; without, it is the point or sphere of S1.
`Motion` gains `Modes`: `centre + amplitude · Σ scale_m cos(2π f_m t + φ_m)` (up to 64 modes),
the scaffold's scalar `α(t)`; a body that moves this way is George's displaced member. The engine
keeps each body's tree for drawing in a cache bounded by bytes; the read builds the tree it needs
and drops it (cav-sim holds no state between calls).

### Drawn as trees, coloured by a field

The renderer draws a voxel body's leaves as cubes (at most 20,000 a view; past that, the cut one
level up until it fits, and the badge says which level is drawn). `colour <field>` picks the
field: `level`, `mass`, `height`, `distance` (to the nearest tower), `phase` (each leaf's share of
a chosen tower's ΔΦ for a static body), `material`. Sequential and diverging colour maps are
added to `cav-plot`'s palettes, checked as the categorical one is; the legend's range, ticks and
unit are the drawn values' own. `figure view` writes the 3-D view as SVG and PDF (each visible
face a polygon, painter's order, orthographic) or PNG.

### Per-tower instruments (TASK-S3-08)

The S15 notes' design: `TowerSpec.instrument: Option<InstrumentOverride>` (every field of
`InstrumentSpec` as an option, `None` the array's), `ArraySpec::instrument_of(tower)`; readers
shared by instrument key (every field by its bits); each tower's shots from the array's schedule
at its own cycle and 2T; `Shots` and `Signal` become per tower (CSR: flat values and `D + 1`
offsets), and the bundle writes the CSR arrays and the padded `(T_max, D)` view with `mask` and
`n_shots`, padded readouts NaN and padded values 0. Zero towers is a valid scene: every place the
reviewer listed (`plan/PROGRESS.md`, item 8 of the slice's prompt) is found again and made to work.

### 3.3 Criteria written before measuring

- **R2.** Every node's mass, centre of mass and second and third central moments, computed
  bottom-up, against the direct sum over its leaves: ≤ 1e-13 of the node's own scale (mass; mass ×
  side; mass × side²; mass × side³).
- **R4.** 0 cells differ in count or material, at every pitch, `k`, and placement tried.
- **R5.** `|Σm − M| ≤ 1e-12·M`. At 64 finest cells across the shape's longest side, `k = 4`:
  the voxelised volume (Σ count × (h/k)³) within 2e-3 of the analytic, and each element of the
  inertia tensor (about the centre of mass, the body's mass) within 5e-3 of the largest principal
  moment. The error at 128 cells is at least three times smaller than at 32 (it converges); the
  order is measured and recorded, not assumed.
- **R6.** For every primitive and Platonic solid at 32 cells across: the potential at 24 points
  (from a tenth of the body's size outside its surface to ten sizes away) and one shot's δφ₁, δφ₂
  and ΔΦ for a tower 1 to 3 m away, merged against unmerged: ≤ 1e-12 of the largest of |δφ₁|,
  |δφ₂| (and of |V| for the potentials). Control: merging eight children whose counts differ by
  one (as if equal) moves the reading by more than 1e-9 of it.
- **R7.**
  - The series to each order `L` ∈ {0, 4, 8, 12} against a reference (Gauss–Legendre, 24 points an
    axis, compensated sums, for `R ≥ 1.2·a`) over `s/R` from 0.02 to 0.3 and 64 directions: the
    error never above the bound.
  - The kernel (series or prism as chosen) against the same reference from `s/R` = 10⁻⁴ to 0.8,
    and against the prism in 40-digit arithmetic (a fixture made once with mpmath) at points inside
    and on the cube: ≤ 1e-13 of |V|. If the prism misses it near the switch, the series gains
    terms; the criterion does not move.
  - `(V_cube − V_point)/V_point` on the axis over `s/R` from 0.01 to 0.1: a log–log slope of
    4.00 ± 0.02 and the coefficient `7/480` within 1% (the leading term of the series, derived in
    `reference/design/voxel.md` §5.2). The order is a validation case.
- **R9.** A voxel sphere of radius R at pitch R/16 (k = 4), a tower 2 m from its centre: its
  reading within 2e-3 of the analytic sphere's (the point mass's), and within 1e-3 at R/32.
- **R12, each against George's script, fixtures made by `scripts/reference_fixtures.py` as S1's
  were:**
  - `cuboid_potential_matches_script`: the script's potential (its `nquad`) at its own grid of
    points (50 × 50: the wall's x₀ against the atom's height) against (a) the prism kernel and (b) the
    wall voxelised on a grid that fits its faces (7.5 cm: 3 × 80 × 160 cells, merged): ≤ 1e-10
    relative at every point. (Measured before writing this, as a check of the reference only:
    the script's `nquad` is within 1.1e-14 of the prism in 40-digit arithmetic at five points.)
    Then the script's phase series against the engine's same series: within the script's own
    error, which the harness measures by running its pipeline again on a doubled grid; the
    criterion is three times that change.
  - `cylinder_vs_point_mass`: for each height h₀ and height H the script sweeps (a subset, to keep
    the fixture to an hour), the ratio of the cylinder's largest AC |ΔΦ| to the point mass's, from
    the engine (voxel cylinder at R/16) and from the script: within 5e-3 of each other. The
    crossover, where the ratio leaves 1 by 10%, is found in each and recorded; the plan's reading
    ("passes 10% when the body's height passes about 10% of the 10 m baseline") is held as: the
    engine's crossover at every h₀ between 0.5 and 2 m. If the scripts say otherwise, that is a
    finding for George, not a test to loosen.
  - `planar_mass` (no script): a horizontal plate under a tower, oscillating vertically. (a) The
    voxel plate on a grid that fits its faces against the prism kernel: ≤ 1e-12 of |δφ|. (b) As the
    plate grows (2, 8, 32, 128 m across, the same areal density) the gradiometer's AC amplitude
    falls towards the infinite slab's exact null; the fall is measured and recorded (S1's
    `finite_floor` case is the static analogue).
  - `scaffold_single_mode`: George's 48 steel members, each a voxel cylinder moving as
    `A_i·α(t)` in x. (a) The engine's potential change `V(α, z) − V(0, z)` at the script's grid
    against the script's: within 1e-2 of its largest value (voxelised rods of 3.4 cells' radius).
    (b) The displaced-body read (every member read directly, node by node) against the engine's
    table method (one `V(α)` per flight node, interpolated in α): ≤ 1e-6 of the largest AC
    |ΔΦ|. (c) The engine's series against the script's: within three times the change in the
    script's own series when its grid is doubled (the harness measures it).
  - `scaffold_multimode_scalar_trick`: the same three with the eight modes of
    `scaffold_multimode`; (b) is the plan's assertion: the displaced-body path agrees with the
    table method.
- **R13.** 0 bits differ between each tower of a mixed array and a one-tower scene of that tower's
  resolved instrument, position and offset, for every shot, at 1 and 6 threads. Controls: one
  tower's T changed by one ulp changes that tower's bits and no other's; an override equal to the
  default gives the bits of no override and builds one reader; swapping two towers' instruments

- **The palette (§3.2, item 1).** CIEDE2000 ≥ 12 between every pair of the hues under all seven

## 8. After the review (24 Nov 2026)

A fresh review found six important and thirteen minor faults. Each is fixed in a task of its own
(TASK-S3-20 onwards; TASK-S3-19 is the octree depth view, built alongside). This section is
written as the work goes: each criterion below was written before what it judges was measured.

### 8.1 Criteria written before measuring

- **The cuboid series (finding 1).** The script's series is its rule, not the engine's: its arms
  are tables a step ahead of the wall (the one-step offset S1 found in `fig2_*`), joined linearly
  (`interp1d`), integrated by `quad` over `[t_k − 1, t_k − 1 + 2T]`, with the potential from a
  spline of its `nquad` table. The engine is held to that rule as S1 holds it (`oracle.script_*`):
  - *the copy* (`oracle.script_cuboid_copy`): the rule copied (the arms at `(j + 1)·dt` against
    the wall at `j·dt`, linear between, the script's window, each step integrated exactly by
    Gauss–Legendre, the closed-form prism in place of the spline) against the fixture's AC
    series: within three times the script's own error as the brief defined it, its AC change when
    its table is doubled (3.57e-8 of the AC peak): **≤ 1.1e-7 of the AC peak**. Control: the same
    rule without the offset is more than 1e-4 away.
  - *the limit* (`oracle.script_cuboid_limit`): the rule with the offset gone on the whole flight
    at dt/128 against the engine's read of the voxel wall: **≤ 1e-5 of the AC peak**. Why: the
    read is audited to `max(1e-10 rad, 1e-12·|ΔΦ|)` a shot, 1.15e-10 rad at the wall's 114.5 rad,
    so two shots' AC may differ by 2.3e-10 rad, 8e-6 of the 2.86e-5 rad peak; what is left of the
    rule at dt/128 is far smaller (S1: 1e-9 of the peak on AION-10). Control: the published rule
    (the offset, dt) is more than 1e-3 away.
  - *the gap* (`oracle.script_cuboid_series`, which held engine − fixture to 3.48e-2 after it was
    measured): now the engine minus the fixture against what the rule predicts for it (the limit
    minus the copy), shot by shot, as a fraction of the largest predicted difference, two-sided:
    **≤ 0.02**, S1's bound for the same relation. As in S1 it is the limit rescaled given the
    copy, not an independent check; it says how much of the 6.2e-3 the offset is.
- **The scaffold series (finding 2).** The scripts' published series are their `nquad` noise
  (their peak AC is 1.85e-6 rad where the tight integrand gives 5.1e-10), so a case that compared
  the engine with them could not fail. `oracle.script_scaffold_series` and
  `oracle.script_scaffold_multimode_series` now compare the engine with the scripts' own pipeline
  (their spline, their `interp1d` arms a step ahead, their `quad`) run on their integrand
  integrated tightly (`scripts/script_pipeline.py`, rewritten from the scripts and tied to them:
  run with its `quad` on the scripts' own table it gives their published series to 1e-9 of its
  peak). The engine's side is the same sampling (the copy above) on the engine's 48 voxel members
  (1 cm, as case (a)) through the table method's Chebyshev polynomial in α. Held to **three times
  the pipeline's own change at its last doubling of grid, plus 1e-3 for the members'
  voxelisation** (case (a) holds their potential change to 2.3e-5 of its largest at 1 cm), as a
  fraction of the AC peak. Control: the engine against the published series is more than half
  its peak away (the published series is noise).
  - *Restated before the engine was compared, after the reference was measured:* first written as
    "the pipeline on the scripts' own grid (12 α × 30 heights) with its `quad`, against its change
    on a doubled grid (24 × 60)". Run, that reference is no reference: doubling its grid moves it
    by 117% of its peak (single mode) and 20% (multimode), because 30 heights over 10.5 m do not
    resolve rods 0.5 m off the axis, and its `quad` is 1331 and 1.2 times the peak off exact
    integration of the same integrand (on the cuboid, 2.5e-5 of the phase). A criterion of three
    times that could not fail. So the pipeline's `quad` is replaced by exact integration (as for
    the cuboid, §8.2), and its grid is refined in height (30, 60, …, 480 at 24 α; α doubled at the
    finest) until a doubling moves it little; the reference is the finest, and its last doubling
    sets the tolerance.
- **Volume and inertia (finding 5).** The voxelised volume counts sample points, `δ = h/k` apart.
  Each sample point stands for its own cube of side δ; only cubes the surface crosses can be
  miscounted, each by at most δ³, and they lie within `√3·δ/2` of the surface. So for a body of
  surface A and volume V, `|ΔV|/V ≤ √3·A·δ/V` (to first order in δ; Steiner's formula gives the
  rest). A is each shape's own area in closed form (first written as its bounding box's area,
  which bounds it for a convex shape; tightened to the shape's own, the stricter, while the
  control below was being made to fail). Each inertia element (about the centre of mass, the body's mass M)
  moves by at most the miscounted volume times `2·r²_max` (the shape's own second moment and the
  renormalised mass), so `|ΔI_ij|/I_max ≤ 2·M·r²_max·√3·A·δ/(V·I_max)`, `r_max` the box's
  half-diagonal. These are first order in h/k: what a flat face at an arbitrary lattice phase
  gives, and so the right bound for the cuboid, the cylinder (its ends) and the Platonic solids.
  - *`voxel.volume_and_inertia`* (q, 64 across, k = 4): every primitive and Platonic solid's
    volume and inertia errors within those envelopes; the case reports the largest error as a
    fraction of its envelope, **≤ 1**. Control: the same shapes counted by whole cells break the
    envelope for at least one shape. (First written as "a cell in when at least half its samples
    are"; that rounding does not break so loose an envelope, so the control is a cell in when any
    of its samples is, as a voxeliser that ignored its counts would be. The criterion is unchanged.)
  - *The oracle's convergence* (`oracle/tests/bodies.rs`, 32, 64, 128 across): the faceted shapes
    within their envelopes at every size; the curved ones (sphere, ellipsoid), whose error is
    lattice-point counting in a smooth convex body, at least order 1.5 (the classical bound: the
    count's error grows at most as the surface's square root of the volume, `(δ/R)^{3/2}`
    relative), as the root mean square over eight random sub-cell placements so that one lucky or
    unlucky phase does not decide it: **RMS at 128 at most an eighth of RMS at 32**, volume and
    inertia. The single-placement numbers (and "order 0.97", the worst over all shapes, which
    mixed the faceted ones in) are withdrawn from §7.3.
