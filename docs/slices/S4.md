## 1. What the owner can newly do

- **Put static matter in a world.** A scene can have a `world`: terrain, buildings, a road and
  slabs as pieces of one world volume, each of a material (density, colour), voxelised at one pitch
  into chunks of 32³ cells, each chunk a voxel tree with its own palette. Bodies stay as they were
  (their own trees, their own frames).
- **Read it.** The world is a source like a body: each tower reads the chunks of its neighbourhood
  leaf by leaf, exactly, and the chunks farther out through their mass moments, with the bound on
  that approximation in radians folded into the read's error. A chunked box reads as the same box
  as a voxel body; a large slab converges to the infinite floor's null.
- **Fly over it.** Near the camera the world is drawn as greedy-meshed faces, culled on the GPU;
  farther out by ray-marching bricks of a clipmap whose rings coarsen by two each, chosen by
  screen-space error. Chunks stream in around the camera and around every tower, from a region-file
  cache on disk when one is set (natively; in a page the cache is memory only, said on the page).
- **Measure it.** `stress-1e10` (1 km² of terrain at 5 cm with buildings and a road: 10¹⁰ finest
  cells) and `flythrough` (a scripted camera over it) with a frame-time trace and what this card
  holds.
- **Draw it on paper.** `figure paper [plan | section <x|y> <m>]` writes a technical drawing as SVG
  (and PDF): ink outlines, hatched sections, a scale bar, a north arrow, dimension lines and a title
  block.

## 2. Design in one page

**Storage (`cav_core::voxel::world`).** A chunk is 32³ finest cells (`CHUNK_DEPTH` 5), keyed by
`ChunkKey` (three `i32`), its corner at `origin + 32·pitch·key`. Its matter is a `VoxTree` on that
grid (the note §8: "the same tree type"), its leaves' materials indices into the chunk's palette
(at most 255 world materials, a chunk lists those it holds). A leaf's mass is `samples ×
(pitch³/k³) × density`: the world's mass is not `unit × samples` because a chunk may hold several
materials, so the tree's own moments are not used for world chunks; `Chunk::moments` sums the
leaves' cubes with the right densities. Several pieces may overlap: a sample point belongs to the
first piece (in the spec's order) that holds it; a finest cell that two materials share takes the
material with more sample points and all its samples (the volume is kept, the density of the minority
part is that of the majority: an error of at most one cell layer at an interface, visual and
mass both, said in the schema). Chunks are made top-down with `cover` as bodies are (the note's
lossless merge), with no raster of the world ever made.

**Region files (`cav_core::voxel::region`).** A region is 8³ chunks: a header (magic, version, the
world's hash, 512 slots of offset and length) and each chunk's bytes (palette, leaves). Every
count read from a file is checked against its cap before anything is reserved; a file of another
world (hash) or version is not read. Pure bytes here; the disk is `cav-engine`'s
(`world/cache.rs`), natively only.

**The read (`cav_sim::world`, source `World`).** Per tower: the chunks within
`world.read.radius` of the tower's base, nearest first, at most `MAX_READ_CHUNKS`. A chunk whose
bounding radius `a` (about its centre of mass) is at most `θ = world.read.theta` times its least
distance `R` to the tower's atom path is read through its moments (mass, centre, second and third
central moments: the octupole expansion); the others leaf by leaf with the exact cube kernel. The
remainder after the octupole, for matter within `a` of the centre, has a gradient at most
`Σ_{l≥4} (l+1) Σ m s^l / R^{l+2}` (each term `s^l P_l(cos γ)/R^{l+1}` has a gradient of at most
`(l+1) s^l/R^{l+2}`, from `P_l² + (1 − x²)P_l'²/(l(l+1)) ≤ 1`; each `Σ m s^l` summed from the
leaves to l = 16, the fourth exactly, and geometrically in `a` past it: TASK-S4-12, after the
review's I1; the first version took `√((l+1)(2l+1))` and `Σ m s^l ≤ Σ m s⁴ · a^{l−4}`); so its
share of `V(upper) − V(lower)` is at most `|upper − lower|` times that. Read as a source of its own, with the quadrature's positive weights, it gives a bound in
radians on each interferometer, which is added to the read's error estimate. The world is static,
so each tower's reading is made once (as a still voxel body's is). Matter outside the read radius
or past the chunk cap is not read: the read says so (S5's bounded walk reads the rest).

**Streaming (`cav-engine` `world.rs`).** The engine keeps the world's resident chunks: those of
ring 0 around each scene pane's camera and those within `world.stream.tower_radius` of each tower
(the instrument's neighbourhood, always resident), and the bricks of each ring. Each `poll` (and
each command) builds at most `MAX_BUILDS_PER_POLL` from a queue ordered by distance (bounded,
deduplicated); a chunk is built from the cache if it is there, else voxelised and written to the
cache. Residents past the cap are dropped farthest first. The page builds the same way, on its one
thread, within the same per-poll count.

**Near field (`cav-render` `world.rs`).** Each resident chunk of ring 0 is greedy-meshed (exposed
faces only, merged into rectangles per material, the neighbours' faces looked up so a buried face
is not drawn; a missing neighbour counts as air). A compute pass tests every chunk's box against
the camera's frustum and writes each chunk's indirect draw (instance count 0 or 1), and each chunk
is drawn with `draw_indexed_indirect` (no multi-draw and no `first_instance`: neither is core in
WebGPU), its camera-relative origin by a dynamic uniform offset formed in f64 on the CPU (§3.4).
Hierarchical-Z occlusion is not built (§6).

**Far field and the clipmap.** Level `k` of the clipmap is a cube of `2n` bricks around the
camera, a brick 32 cells of `2^k · pitch`; the level boxes nest exactly (`c_0 = 2⌊cam/2b_0⌋`,
`c_k = 2⌊c_{k−1}/4⌋`), and a level draws only the bricks outside the next finer level's box.
Level 0 is the near field; levels 1 and up are ray-marched. `n` comes from the screen-space
error: a cell at the inner edge of level `k` subtends about `1/(16 n θ_px)` pixels, so
`n = ⌈1/(16 θ_px ε)⌉` for `ε` = `world.lod.error_px` pixels, capped. The number of levels is what
reaches the world's extent, at most `MAX_LEVELS` (12: S14 extends the same rings outwards). A
brick is 32³ occupancy bits and a 4³ mask of its 8³ sub-blocks (the two-level "64-tree" form:
empty space is skipped a sub-block at a time) and a material per sub-block; a cell is occupied when
`cover` says its box is not outside some piece (conservative: a coarse brick holds every finer
brick's cells, and may add some). The ray-march draws each brick's box and marches it in the
fragment shader: a DDA over the 4³ mask, then over the 8³ cells of an occupied sub-block, at most
`MAX_MARCH_STEPS` steps, writing depth so it composes with the near field. The march is one WGSL
function shared by the fragment shader and a compute kernel, so its CPU twin is held to the GPU.

**Paper (`cav-plot` `paper.rs`).** A plan (from above) or a section (a vertical cut) of the
scene, orthographic: the world's matter as ink outlines of its occupancy at the drawing's
resolution (in plan, the outline of each height band; in section, the cut matter hatched at 45°
and outlined), the towers and bodies, a scale bar of a round length, a north arrow, dimension lines
(the world's extent; the towers' spacing), and a title block (scene, view, scale, the revision).
Written through the figure pipeline, so SVG and PDF come from one display list.

### 7.6 Departures and calls worth a second look

- One PR for TASK-S4-02, 03 and 04: the clipmap, the streaming and both renderers share one data
  path (`WorldFrame`), and none is testable without the others.
- The world's read is bounded by a radius around each tower (default 100 m; `stress-1e10` 20 m at
  `read.tolerance` 1e-3 rad); farther matter is not read and the read says so. Far chunks inside
  the radius go by their moments only when their rigorous bound fits the budget: at the default
  1e-10 rad that is almost never (a 10-tonne chunk needs to be ~2 km away), so the default read is
  leaf-exact.
