## 1. What the owner can newly do

- **Read the whole world.** A scene's world is one octree: its chunks (level 0) merged upward
  into coarser nodes (level 1, 2, …: the same `(level, key)` as S4's bricks), each node carrying
  its mass moments and the absolute moments that bound what they leave out. Each tower reads it by
  a Barnes–Hut walk opened by phase error on **one global ledger**: the node with the largest bound
  is opened first until the bounds sum to `world.read.tolerance` radians, every node nearer than its
  own size is always opened, and opened chunks are read leaf by leaf (the bubble). The far nodes'
  field along the tower's line is gathered once into a **local expansion** (a Chebyshev series in
  height, the FMM step specialised to a vertical gradiometer), cached for the static world. No
  radius, no truncation: the read says "within ε rad", with the ledger beside it.
- **See the levels a tower reads.** A pane's world can be drawn at the levels its camera wants
  (S4's clipmap, as today), at the levels tower *n* reads (its cut), or at the finest level any of
  several towers reads (the union, a common refinement): `lod-view camera | tower 2 | towers 1 2 5`.
  An overlay colours each node by its level, by the tower that set it, or by its share of the
  ledger; `overlay octree` draws the world's nodes by level. Ring seams in the far field are hidden
  by a dithered cross-fade weighted by the camera's distance.
- **Infinite worlds.** `world.base: Plane` is an infinite flat slab (analytic: uniform `g`, no
  gradient) with the pieces read as departures from it (a building adds, a ditch of air carves);
  `world.base: Sphere` is a layered planet (each layer a point mass by the shell theorem) with the
  pieces read as departures from the reference sphere. Both are drawn.
- **Exact gravity of polyhedra.** `cav_core::polyhedral`: Werner & Scheeres' closed form, face by
  face, written with the rounding rules of [X] §1.1 (the solid angle's numerator from the face's own
  edges, `log1p`, the `r₀²` form beside an edge). A body that is a convex polyhedron, a Platonic
  solid or a mesh can be read **exactly** (`representation: Exact`), with no voxels.
- **Exact skins on voxel bodies.** `representation` chooses what a boundary cell carries: `Sampled`
  (today's count of `k³` samples, the default, unchanged to the bit), `Fill` (the exact fill
  fraction at the cell's centre), `Centroid` (fill and the piece's centroid: cut-cell moments),
  `Second` (and the piece's second moment), `Pieces` (the exact clipped piece read by the
  polyhedral kernel near an arm, `Second` beyond). Pieces are exact to the mesh for polyhedra and
  meshes; for spheres, ellipsoids, cylinders and shells the moments come from the analytic surface
  ([X] §3.1's (c★), (d★): no mesh anywhere).
- **Draw a body four ways.** `draw voxels | fill | mesh | mesh-over-voxels | pieces`: the cells as
  today; the cells shaded by their exact fill with a legend; the body's own triangles; both; each
  boundary cell as its clipped polyhedron.

## 2. Design in one page

**The polyhedral kernel (`cav-core/src/polyhedral.rs`).** For a uniform closed polyhedron of density
`ρ` (faces outward, each a triangle or a convex polygon), at a point `p`, with `r = v − p`:
`U/G = ½ρ Σ_f h_f (S_f − h_f ω_f)` and `g/G = −ρ Σ_f n_f (S_f − h_f ω_f)`, `h_f = n_f·r`,
`S_f = Σ_e (n_e·r_e) L_e`, `L_e = log1p(2ℓ/(a + b − ℓ))` with `a + b − ℓ` in the `r₀²` form when the
point projects onto the edge, and `ω_f = 2 atan2(h_f·2A_f, r₁r₂r₃ + r₁ r₂·r₃ + r₂ r₃·r₁ + r₃ r₁·r₂)`
per triangle ([X] §1.1, W&S eqs. 10, 15, 27). Faces are stored as `(n, 2A, vertices)`. Degenerate
places (on a face, an edge, a vertex) drop the terms whose factor vanishes; `Σ ω_f` is `0` outside
and `4π` inside, asserted. The sign convention is the repository's: `V = −G∫ρ/r` (negative), so the
kernel returns `−U`. Beyond `R/s = EXACT_WITHIN` (10³, [X] §1.1 rule 2) a body is read through its
exact moments (by the divergence theorem over the same faces: volume, centroid, second and third
moments) and the octupole expansion with S4's bound; nearer by the closed form. The forward model
reads `V(upper) − V(lower)`; for the closed form that difference is of two potentials of the same
size, so the switch is set where the difference keeps its digits (measured, §7).

**Cut-cell moments (`cav-core/src/voxel/cut.rs`).** A boundary cell's piece is clipped exactly:
- *convex polyhedra* (`Cuboid`, `Polyhedron`, `Platonic`): the cell cube clipped by each face's
  half-space (Sutherland–Hodgman on a polyhedron, r3d's way);
- *meshes*: by the signed vertical-prism decomposition: for a closed mesh, the indicator at `x` is
  the signed count of triangles above `x` (`+1` where the surface faces up, `−1` down), so
  `cell ∩ body = Σ_t sign(n_z) · (cell ∩ prism below t)`, each term the cube clipped by four
  half-spaces (the triangle's three vertical side planes and its own plane). Only triangles whose
  shadow meets the cell's square and that reach above its floor take part; they are found from a
  2-D bin of the triangles' shadows. Exact to rounding, and robust where faces lie on lattice
  planes;
- *analytic curved shapes* (`Sphere`, `Ellipsoid` by scaling, `Shell` as a difference, `Cylinder`
  by an axis permutation): the z-chord integrated exactly, `(x, y)` by Gauss–Legendre on pieces split
  at every kink with a cosine map at each end ([X] §3.1, `shapes/analytic.py` ported).
Each piece gives its volume, centroid and central second moment (and, for `Pieces`, its faces). A
tree gains a side table `cuts` (key, volume, centroid, second), sorted, its bytes counted in the
tree's budget; a cut leaf weighs `ρ × volume`, and node moments are built from the exact pieces, so
the walk's multipoles are the true body's ([X] §5 "the octree merge"). `count` stays, for the
picture and the `Fill` colour of a `Sampled` body. The raster oracle stays for `Sampled`; for the
others the oracle is "the pieces' moments sum to the shape's closed-form moments" ([X] §5 item 2).

**Reading a cut cell.** `Fill`: a cube of the cell at its centre (the cube kernel). `Centroid`: a
cube of the cell's side at the piece's centroid. `Second`: a point at the centroid with the piece's
quadrupole, and within `PRISM_WITHIN` cells the `Centroid` cube (finite inside). `Pieces`: within
`PIECE_WITHIN` (3) cells of the arm the clipped polyhedron by the polyhedral kernel, `Second`
beyond ([X] §5 item 3). `Exact`: the whole body by the polyhedral kernel ([X] §4 (a)), capped at
`MAX_EXACT_FACES`.

**The world octree (`cav-sim/src/world/octree.rs`).** Node `(L, key)` is S4's brick key: a cube of
`32·2^L` cells. Its `Summary`: raw moments about the node's own **geometric centre** (mass, first,
second, third: additive, so signed departures and edits add exactly, [O] §3), the absolute
moments `A_k = Σ|m| s^k` for `k = 0 … 16` about the same centre, and its reach `e` (all matter
within `e` of the centre). A chunk's summary is summed from its cubes (`A_2`, `A_4` exact for a
cube, the rest at each cube's far corner). A parent's raw moments are its children's shifted
(binomial, exact algebra) and summed in octant order; its `A_l` is the smaller of
`Σ_c Σ_k C(l,k) A_k(c) δ_c^{l−k}` (triangle inequality, [O] §2 table) and `A_0 e^l`. Built
bottom-up from every chunk with matter, in parallel by chunk and deterministic by key order; at
most `MAX_READ_CHUNKS_WHOLE` chunks and `MAX_WORLD_NODES` nodes, counted first.

**The walk (`cav-sim/src/world/walk.rs`).** Per tower, the target is its atoms' path, the vertical
segment from the base up `tower_height`. A node's bound `B` is S4's: the remainder after the
octupole has a gradient at most `Σ_{l≥4} (l+1) A_l / R^{l+2}`, times the arm span, times the
flight's radians per kg/m, with `R` the least distance from the node's centre to the segment
(minus nothing: the `A_l` are about that centre). `B = ∞` when `e/R ≥ Q_MAX` (½): such a node is
opened whatever the tolerance ([O] §0, S&W's footnote 3). From the root(s), a priority queue on
`B`: pop the largest, replace it by its children, until `Σ B ≤ τ` (S&W's Sum MAC, [O] §2.1). An
opened chunk is read leaf by leaf with the cube kernel (`B = 0`): the bubble. `world.read.bubble`
(metres, default 0) forces every chunk within it open. The cut is an antichain covering each chunk
once (the partition test). The ledger is the accepted nodes' bounds read through the flight (S4's
`BoundSource`, the quadrature's weights positive). Cut nodes past `MAX_CUT_NODES` stop the walk:
the read is then **not settled** and says by how much its ledger exceeds `τ` (never truncated
silently).

**The local expansion (`cav-sim/src/world/local.rs`).** Every arm point of a tower lies on one
vertical line. The far field `D(z) = Σ_nodes [V_n(z) − V_n(z₀)]` (each term by the
cancellation-free difference, so `D` keeps its digits) is interpolated at Chebyshev points on the
arm span, the degree doubled from 16 until the last coefficients are below the expansion's share of
`τ`, at most `MAX_LOCAL_DEGREE` (256); then audited against direct evaluation at the midpoints. The
flight integral reads `D(upper) − D(lower)` plus the bubble's leaves. The audit (the larger of the
tail and the measured mismatch) joins the ledger. Cached per tower with the world (it is static).

**Measured radii ([O] §5, TASK-S5-14).** `cav bench lod-radii --scene <s>` measures, for each level
`L` and tolerance, the nearest distance (in node sides) at which the ledger walk accepts a level-`L`
node, and the protocol of [O] §5 (level by level against the leaf-exact read, the worst over ≥ 8
tower positions). The table goes in §7. `world.read.walk: Ledger | Rings` chooses the walk: the
ledger (default) or rings at the measured radii (a cheaper cut whose ledger is reported the same
way, valid only where its table reproduces `τ`: the gate of [O] §5).

**Infinite bases (`world.base`).** `Patch` (today's finite world, the default), `Plane { surface,
density, thickness }`, `Sphere { radius, layers }` (planet centre at `(0, 0, −radius)`). The base is
read in closed form (the plane: S1's `Slab`, uniform `g`, zero gradient; the sphere: each layer a
point mass at the centre; towers must stand above the surface). Pieces are departures: a chunk's
matter is its pieces' cubes plus a second tree, the **overlap** of the pieces with the base, at
minus the base's density (one code path for both bases; cells cut by a curved surface weighed by
their samples, as pieces are). A piece may not reach below the top layer. Drawn: the plane as the
infinite floor is, the sphere as a curved grid to the horizon.

**Draw modes and LOD views (`cav-sim/src/voxel_view.rs`, `world/lod_view.rs`, `cav-render`).** A
view's `body_draw` and `lod_view` are view settings (journalled, never undone, as camera moves).
`lod_view: Tower(n)` draws the engine's cut for tower `n` as the draw list (level 0 as meshes,
levels ≥ 1 as bricks), with no new walk ([O] §7a); `Towers([..])` the common refinement (strict
ancestors of any cut node are opened, the rest emitted; ties of the tower overlay to the lowest
id). A cut node with no brick resident draws its nearest resident ancestor and is counted in the
badge.

**Seams ([O] §7b).** In the brick march, over a band inset one snap step inside each finer ring's
outer edge, each pixel keeps the finer or the coarser level by a 4×4 Bayer threshold on
`α = clamp((d − d₀)/w)`, `d` the continuous camera distance in the ring's own cells, `w` 10 % of the
ring's half-width and at least two coarse cells. Position-driven, so frames are reproducible.

- Speed: 48.5 ns per face per point for the potential (with `g` and `Σω`, which cost almost
  nothing extra), 4096 faces; the difference (two points) 92 to 98 ns. [X]'s estimate was 55 ns.
  Each edge's logarithm and each vertex's distance are computed once per point and shared by the
  two faces of an edge, which halves the logarithms and square roots of a face-by-face loop.
- `EXACT_WITHIN` = 10³ sizes holds for `V` and `g` (8e-10 there). **The difference
  `V(upper) − V(lower)` by the closed form keeps its digits much less far**: against the cube
  kernel's cancellation-free difference, a 1 m cube, gap = s: 2.7e-11 at R/s = 10, 2.4e-10 at
  30, 1.9e-8 at 100, 6.9e-6 at 10³; gap = 10 s: 5.3e-10 at 100, 1.6e-6 at 10³. So 1e-9 holds to
  about **30 sizes for a gap of one size and 100 for ten**, an error of about `ε(R/s)²·R/gap`.
  TASK-S5-06 should switch the forward model's difference to the moments there, not at 10³; the
  constant stays 10³ as the brief gives it, for `V` and `g`.
- P1's `g` reference is the exact prism attraction (Nagy's corner term differentiated, written in
  the test), itself checked against a five-point difference of `prism_potential` (≤ 1e-8).
- P3's reference: [X]'s `2 asinh(ℓ/2d)` for `L`, and Nagy's prism and its attraction for `V` and
  `g` at the same point, rather than "a series in `d`" about 1e-10 m: the second derivatives are
  log-singular at an edge, so no short series in `d` reaches 1e-10 at 1e-4 m, and the prism is
  an exact closed form that does not cancel there. The cube sits near the origin (centre
  ≈ 0.015 m, non-dyadic) because the floor from rounding the point's own position, `ε|p|/(d·L)`,
  is estimated at 3e-10 for [X]'s 0.4 m at 1e-8 m (2e-11 here, measured 4.2e-12).
- P4: a general point on an edge is within rounding of the edge's line, not on it, so its `L` is
  large (≈ 76) but finite and its factor is ~ε; only a vertex gives an infinite `L`, dropped.
- A point within `8ε(|p| + max|v|)` of a face's plane has `ω_f = 0` (the mean of its two
  one-sided limits): that is what makes `Σω` 2π on a face; `U` and `g` are unchanged by it
  (`h_f ω_f` and `h_f² ω_f` vanish there).
- `size` is twice the farthest vertex from the centroid (does not depend on how the solid is
  turned); for a cube it is `s√3`.
- Moments in two passes (the centroid, then central moments about it) so nothing is moved by
  the parallel-axis rules; the third moments by the Dirichlet moments of a tetrahedron's
  barycentric coordinates.
- Constructors: `new` (loose triangles, corners joined by their bits), `indexed`,
  `from_polyhedron` (fan-triangulated), `platonic`, `from_cuboid`, `from_mesh`; each checks that
  every edge is in exactly two triangles once each way and that the volume is positive, and caps at
  `MAX_FACES` (a mesh's `MAX_TRIANGLES`) before reserving.

- **Town** (release): 5,454 chunk keys searched in 1 ms. 5,350 have matter, with 3.29 M leaves.
  Nodes per level are 5,350, 1,274, 320, 86, 26 and 8 (7,064 in all). It builds in 0.82 s on 8
  threads and 4.65 s on 1, holds 2.51 MB (352 B a node) and weighs 1.4704e8 kg. A debug build
  takes 0.9 to 1.6 s, because the voxeliser is in `cav-core`, which is optimised in dev too. So
  only the town test is in the slow tier.
- **Caps.** `MAX_READ_CHUNKS_WHOLE` = 131,072, 24 times `town`'s chunks. While the levels are
  made, their summaries take at most 43 MB (328 B each). `MAX_WORLD_NODES` = 180,000 covers a
  world of one layer of chunks, as terrain is, which has 4/3 nodes a chunk: `town` has 1.32. That
  is at most 63 MB. `stress-1e10` (about 4×10⁵ chunks) is refused: reading it whole belongs to the
  walk's streaming, not to this build.
- **Roots.** Levels are made upward until one has at most eight nodes (`MAX_ROOTS`), not up to a
  single root. A world that straddles the lattice's planes through the origin, as every dev world
  centred on the array does, keeps keys −1 and 0 apart at every level, because halving never
  merges them. The town's top is level 5, with 8 roots.
- **Search.** The chunks that may hold matter are found top-down: from the coarsest level at which
  the extent is at most two bricks a side, each brick that `World::reaches` is split into eight.
  The brief asked for every chunk of the extent that `reaches`. A chunk with matter has every
  ancestor reaching, so the chunks with matter are the same, and the search does not visit the
  air. `town` searched 5,454 keys, not the 36,864 of its box.
- **O2's "direct"** is the leaves' true `A_l`, not the leaf rule (far corner) applied about the
  node's centre: even orders are exact (`cube_even_powers`, by the multinomial theorem); odd
  orders are Cauchy–Schwarz on those, which is never below the truth. The leaf rule is itself an
  over-estimate. For a cube lying directly opposite the shift, the carried value at `l = 3` falls
  below it by `3δ(f² − d² − h²/4)` while staying above the truth. Orders 2 and 4 of a chunk are
  the truth itself and are allowed 1e-12 for rounding.
- **O1's reference** is summed with Neumaier's compensation. Summed plainly, the direct sum over a
  level-2 node's 10⁵ cubes is itself 2.9e-13 off, measured, which is more than the octree. The
  tolerance is unchanged. The first terrain world (0.5 m, origin 0) measured exactly 0: on a
  dyadic lattice every product is exact in `f64`. Its pitch and origin were made non-dyadic so
  that the test sees rounding.
- The expansion keeps the dipole, which is not zero about a geometric centre. Its difference is
  formed without cancellation, as the monopole's is: `1/a³ − 1/b³` is formed from `1/a − 1/b`. In
  `cav-core` it is held to the cubes' exact kernel from 1.5 to 60 node sides, its error within the
  bound everywhere. The control (the octupole left out) exceeds the bound at more than half of
  the points.
- `Summary` keeps its centre (24 B), though the key gives it, so that the `cav-core` type stands
  alone and the walk can form each node-to-tower vector from it.
- Chunk trees are dropped once summarised; `octree::chunk_cubes(world, key)` makes one again for
  the walk.
- Row X3's test is `the_octree_is_bounded`, in the quick tier: it counts the callbacks asked to
  show that no chunk was made. No memory was measured; the bytes above are computed from the
  sizes.
- Carried `A_4` is 2.6 to 6.2 times the truth above the chunks. Carrying it exactly is in
  `plan/IDEAS.md`, with its likely gain: about 15 % nearer acceptance for 3× tighter.
