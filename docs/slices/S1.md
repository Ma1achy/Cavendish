## 1. What the owner can newly do

- **Put a mass in the scene and drag it**: `mass 10 at 3 0 1` in the console (or load `point`), select
  it, drag it in a pane. Every tower's phase is read again as it moves; the readings are beside the
  towers in the outliner, on the figure and in the properties.
- **Play a straight pass** (`load pass`, Space): the mass moves, the playhead runs along the lanes, and
  the signal plot draws each tower's series with the playhead on it.
- **Plot the signal** in any pane: `show signal`. **Plot its periodogram**: `show periodogram`, with
  the comb of each oscillating body drawn over it: a stem at every harmonic `n·f₀`, folded about the
  Nyquist frequency where it lies above it, and labelled as folded.
- **Edit anything by path**: `set bodies[0].mass 20`, `set array.instrument.launch_floor false`; undo
  takes it back. `signal`, `reading` and `periodogram` answer in the console, `cav exec` and MCP's
  `command` tool.
- **Load the new dev scenes**: `point`, `pass`, `oscillator-zoo`, `superposition`, `mirror-pair`,
  `switch-on`, `flash`, `time-reversal`, `ring-orbit`, `through-the-tower`, `precision-far`.
- **Run the oracle tier**: `cargo xtask oracle` compiles v1 at `v1-final` and holds the engine to it and
  to George's scripts. `cargo xtask validate --report --against s0-shell` is the slice-end check.

## 2. Design in one page

### The forward model (`cav-core::forward`, f64)

For interferometer `k` of a tower, `δφ_k = (m_A/ħ) ∫₀^2T [V(upper arm) − V(lower arm)] dt`; the
gradiometer reads `ΔΦ = δφ₁ − δφ₂`.

| Choice | The engine's default | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Sign | lower minus upper, `δφ₁ − δφ₂` | George, 21 Nov 2026 |
| Which is interferometer 1 | **the lower one** | confirmed in v1's code (`build_arms` returns `[make(0.0), make(cfg.ifo_sep)]`, and `delta_phi` is `d2 − d1`: upper minus lower) and in the scripts (`lower1`/`upper1` launch from z = 0, `lower2`/`upper2` from z = δr; the series is `δφ₁ − δφ₂`) |
| Timing | atom and source at the same instant | George |
| Atom | ⁸⁷Sr, 86.908 877 5 u × 1.660 539 068 92 × 10⁻²⁷ kg = 1.443 155 87 × 10⁻²⁵ kg | NIST Atomic Weights and Isotopic Compositions; CODATA 2022 (both read 21 Nov 2026; `cav-core::constants`) |
| ħ, G | 1.054 571 817 × 10⁻³⁴ J s (exact SI); 6.674 30 × 10⁻¹¹ | SI 2019; CODATA 2022 |
| Launch | u₀ = 3.86 m/s (AION-10) | George |
| Floor | an arm that falls back to its launch height stops there with no velocity; before T the kick starts it again from rest; after T it waits for the next sequence | George: "coded as the Python scripts code the sequence" (`atom_path`: `if z <= floor and v < 0: z = floor; v = 0; break`) |
| Kick | `v_kick = 1000 ħ k / m_A`, λ = 698 nm: 6.578 m/s with the constants above (6.505 with the references' rounded ones) | scripts and v1 |
| g | 9.81 m/s² | scripts and v1 |

Floor, mass, ħ, G and sign are fields of `Instrument`; the quadrature is a field too
(`Quadrature::{Gauss, Simpson, ScriptTrapezoid}`), and all of them feed **one** integrator: a list of
nodes (source time, weight, the two arms' rise) summed against a `Source`. v1's conventions and the
scripts' are instruments, not code paths: `Instrument::with_v1_conventions()` (no floor, v1's mass and
ħ, upper minus lower, Simpson at 0.01 s on each half) and `Quadrature::ScriptTrapezoid { lag }` (the
scripts' nodes; `lag = 1` as published, `lag = 0` with the offset corrected).

**The mass enters through the trajectories.** `(m_A/ħ)·v_kick = 1000·k` whatever the mass, so in a
uniform gradient the phase does not depend on `m_A` at all. What changes is the arms' reach (the kick
is 1.1% larger) and where they land: with ⁸⁷Sr the AION-10 arms would close 1.8 cm below the launch
height at 2T, not 7.2 cm. For sources a few metres away that is not small: measured on the anchors
with everything else as v1 has it, ⁸⁷Sr's mass moves the phase by 0.5% to 2.7% of its peak (§7.1,
where an earlier wording of this paragraph is corrected).

**The engine's quadrature** is Gauss–Legendre, six nodes a panel, panels of at most 0.1 s, on pieces
cut at every kink: the π pulse, each arm's landing, and every time at which the source is not smooth
(a body appears or vanishes; a pass starts or stops). v1's Simpson rule runs straight through kinks
and is 1e-8 to 3e-7 from the converged integral (research note §3); it is kept only as the rule that
ties the engine to v1.

**Precision rules of §3.4, on the CPU now:** a source's position is turned into an offset from the
tower's base in f64 before anything else; arm heights are the rise above the arm's own launch point;
`1/|a| − 1/|b|` is formed as `(|b|² − |a|²)/(|a||b|(|a| + |b|))` with `|b|² − |a|²` from the arms'
separation; the flight's sum is in kg s/m and `G·m_A/ħ` multiplies it once.

**Sources in S1:** a point mass or uniform sphere on a path (`Mass`); an infinite uniform slab below
the array (`Slab`); a finite disc on a tower's axis (`AxialDisc`, for the edge-term convergence only).
A sphere is a point outside itself and has the closed-form interior potential, so it may cross an
atom's path where a point may not.

### Analysis (`cav-core::spectrum`, f64 on the CPU)

The classical periodogram `S_k = (Δt/N)|FFT(y − ȳ)|²` at `f_k = k/(NΔt)`, one-sided, not doubled,
with the `1D_oscillator` variant (no mean subtraction, DC kept); the exact Lomb–Scargle of the
scripts (Scargle's τ, pre-centred, times `dt_eff`); `fold_frequency`; a least-squares notch; and, if
it fits, the NUFFT Lomb–Scargle (§6 says whether it did). No GPU periodogram (owner's ruling).

### The oracle crate (`oracle/`, outside the workspace)

v1's `instrument`, `gravity` and `source` at tag `v1-final` as a git dependency, compiled only by
`cargo xtask oracle` and `cargo xtask validate`; George's scripts as fixtures
(`oracle/fixtures/scripts/`, made by `scripts/reference_fixtures.py`, provenance beside them).

**REBUILD §11's open question: a dependency on v1, or frozen fixtures?** Decided by measuring, with
the criterion written first: the dependency stays if building v1's crates from nothing costs under
60 s and a warm run of the tier spends under 5 s on them; otherwise the anchors' outputs are frozen
and the dependency is dropped. Either way the anchors' v1 outputs are also frozen as fixtures
(`oracle/fixtures/v1/`), so that `quick` holds the engine to v1 on every commit without compiling v1,
and the oracle tier checks that the frozen numbers are still what v1 computes. The measurement is in
§7.

### 3.1 Criteria written before measuring

- **R7, 1e-10:** v1's rule is reproduced node for node, so what is left is rounding in two different
  orders of the same arithmetic: expected about 1e-13 of peak.
- **R9, 1e-9:** the research note's independent re-implementation got 2e-13 and 5e-16.
- **R10, as first written (3e-4 of peak) and as the review replaced it.** First: "the corrected
  scripts still use the trapezoid rule at 0.01 s (4e-5 to 1e-4 of peak) and stop one step short of 2T
  (estimated 1e-4 for AION-10, 1e-5 for AICE) … the number is not moved". One series then measured
  1.07e-3 and its tolerance was moved to 1.5e-3 with the wrong cause (§7.1). The review measured the
  parts: the window that ends one step short is the main term (8.1e-4 of the 1.07e-3 on
  `100m_oscillators`; 2.7e-4 to 3.2e-4 on `fig2_10m` b, c, d), and seven cases passed 3e-4 only
  because the trapezoid's error has the other sign. **The criterion now, each number written before
  the builder measured it** (the reviewer's own measurements are in brackets):
  - *the tie:* the fixture is the scripts' rule with the offset gone, to 1e-9 of peak;
  - *the limit:* that rule on the whole flight, taken to its limit, is the engine. On AICE, two
    Richardson steps (steps of dt, dt/2, dt/4) to 1e-9 of peak [3e-10 and 5e-12]. On AION-10, where
    the arms land between nodes and the error is not a clean series in the step, the rule at dt/32 to
    5e-7 [4e-8 to 1.4e-7];
  - *the gap, two-sided:* the engine minus the fixture must be the limit minus the scripts' rule at
    their own step and window, shot by shot, to 2% of that difference. Expected: 1e-5 of it on AICE,
    under 0.3% on AION-10. This fails if the engine drifts either way; it cannot pass by
    cancellation.
- **R11, 1e-11:** against the same rule with twice the nodes a panel and panels a quarter as long.
- **R11b:** a source 0.5 m from the atoms changes on a scale of 0.5 m / 10 m/s = 0.05 s, half a
  panel, so six nodes a panel cannot be converged there: the fixed rule is expected to be off by
  1e-8 rad or more, and the audited read to need two or three halvings.
- **R33, R39 to R41, written before the spectra of the fixtures were looked at by the engine.** The
  engine's periodogram of each script's series is the script's own to 1e-9 of the peak. On `fig2_10m`
  panel b without noise, the four largest bins of the engine's own series are the bins of 0.05,
  0.10, 0.15 and 0.20 Hz, and so are the script's. With the script's shot noise (1e-4 rad/√Hz) the
  fixture's fourth harmonic, 71 µrad/√Hz at 2e4 s, is under the floor of 100, so "n = 1…4 above
  the noise" cannot hold at this length: the bins more than six times the median are expected to be
  those of the first three harmonics and no others. For the folds, the four largest bins of panel c
  on both instruments are where `fold_frequency(n·f₀)` puts n = 1…4. For the suppression law, the
  height of the n-th harmonic over the fundamental grows as `A^(n−1)`: slopes of 1 and 2 for n = 2
  and 3, each within 10%, over A from 0.05 to 0.4 m.
- **R13:** above a disc of radius R the leading edge term is a uniform gradient `2πGρh/R`, so the
  gradiometer reads `Δr/R` of what one interferometer reads from the slab: 7.8e-4 at R = 6.4 km.
- **R14, 1e-11:** an offset formed from coordinates of 1e4 m is good to 2e-12 m, 4e-13 of 5 m.
- **R21:** each arm is its own mirror image about T only when both arms close at the launch height
  at 2T, which is the AICE launch rule; AION-10's 3.86 m/s with the floor is not symmetric.

### 3.2 Criteria for the NUFFT Lomb–Scargle (written before measuring)

`lomb_scargle_fast(t, y, df, count)` gives Scargle's periodogram at `f = k·df`, `k = 0 … count − 1`,
from two type-1 non-uniform FFTs (the sums `Σ(y − ȳ)e^{iωt}` and `Σe^{2iωt}`), with the "exponential
of semicircle" kernel (Barnett, Magland & af Klinteberg 2019), upsampling 2, width 14, in `f64`.
What it is held to is the exact `lomb_scargle` at the same frequencies. The error is the largest
`|S_fast − S_exact|` over the frequencies compared, as a fraction of the largest `S_exact`.

1. **Equal to the exact one.** 2000 shots at a cycle of 2 s with up to 0.3 s of jitter (a comb, a
   tone above Nyquist and noise), every frequency from 0 to 0.25 Hz in 1000 steps: error ≤ 1e-9.
   The same on a uniform grid including its Nyquist bin, where a denominator vanishes: ≤ 1e-9.
2. **Long series.** 100 000 jittered shots, 50 000 frequencies computed, 48 of them compared with
   the exact one (the peak's bin, the bins beside it, and the rest spread evenly): error ≤ 1e-9.
   Prediction: the rounding floor of a NUFFT grows as N·ε, here about 2e-11, so this should hold
   with a margin of one to two decades and not more.
3. **Convergence in the kernel's width.** The kernel's error is predicted to fall a decade for each
   grid point of width, about 10^(1−w) of `Σ|c|` at upsampling 2 (FINUFFT's rule `w = ⌈log₁₀ 1/ε⌉ + 1`).
   Held with one decade in hand: at w = 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 the error on case 1's series is ≤ 10^(2−w),
   and each step of two in w gains at least a factor of 10.
4. **Control.** Width 4 fails the 1e-9 of criterion 1 (its error is predicted near 1e-3). A fast
   periodogram whose times are all moved by one second gives the same answer to 1e-9 (the estimator
   does not depend on the origin of time), and one whose times are scaled by 1.001 does not.
5. **Cost.** Stated, not gated: the time for 100 000 shots is recorded in §7.

### 7.3 The NUFFT Lomb–Scargle (TASK-S1-14), against §3.2

Every criterion passed at its first measurement; two of the predictions written beside them were
wrong, both on the cautious side, and are corrected here rather than in §3.2.

| Criterion | Measured | Stated |
|---|---|---|
| 1. 2000 jittered shots, 1001 frequencies | 3.1e-15 of the peak | ≤ 1e-9 |
| 1. uniform grid with its Nyquist bin | 1.1e-15 | ≤ 1e-9 |
| 2. 100 000 jittered shots, 48 of 50 000 frequencies | 4.7e-15 | ≤ 1e-9 |
| 3. width 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 | 1.4e-5, 1.1e-7, 8.5e-10, 5.2e-12, 3.0e-14 | ≤ 10^(2−w); a decade or more per two points |
| 4. width 4 against 1e-9 | 1.4e-5: fails, as a control must | > 1e-9 |
| 4. times moved by 1 s; scaled by 1.001 | same to 1e-9; differs by more than 1e-3 | as stated |
| 5. cost, 100 000 shots and 50 000 frequencies | 0.26 s with the 48 exact checks (one CPU thread) | recorded |

- **The rounding floor is not N·ε here.** §3.2 predicted about 2e-11 at 100 000 shots and "a margin
  of one to two decades and not more"; the margin is five. N·ε is FINUFFT's worst-case bound on a
  sum's relative error; rounding in these sums adds like a random walk (about √N·ε ≈ 7e-14 of
  `Σ|c|`), and the error is taken as a fraction of the peak, which is of order `(Σ|c|)²`.
- **The kernel's error falls about 2.1 decades per two points, and starts two decades lower than
  10^(1−w).** The rule `w = ⌈log₁₀ 1/ε⌉ + 1` bounds the error of a sum against `Σ|c|`; the
  periodogram's error away from its peak is that times the (small) sum there, as a fraction of the
  peak. The criterion 10^(2−w) held with two to three decades to spare at every width; it is left
  as written, not tightened to what was measured.
- It is not wired to a command: nothing in S1 samples unevenly (the engine's periodogram is the
  classical one on its uniform grid). It is there for S11's long, gapped series.
